Curious Buddy: curriculum map

Where Curious Buddy's games, stories, missions and quizzes fit three international primary science curricula, for ages 7 to 11.

25 of 28UK primary science units touched, 13 of them strongly
60+Cambridge Primary Science objectives, Stages 3 to 6
30+US NGSS performance expectations, grades 2 to 5
557reviewed items: facts, questions, clues and story scenes
Safe for school use. Curious Buddy works fully offline. It has no accounts, no ads, no chat with strangers and no data collection, and it never asks a child for personal details. When a child shares a worry, Buddy points them to a trusted grown-up.

How to read this map

  • Part 1, by activity: what each game, story, mission or quiz teaches.
  • Part 2, by lesson: pick a UK unit and see everything that supports it.
  • Part 3, gaps: lessons the app does not cover yet.
  • Items are the numbers on the app's content review page (docs/review/content_review.html), so a teacher can read every fact behind a match.
  • Strong means the activity teaches the idea directly. Some means it introduces or practises part of it. Only clear matches are listed.
  • Curricula used: the National Curriculum in England (science, Years 1 to 6), Cambridge Primary Science 0097 (framework for first teaching from 2021) and the US Next Generation Science Standards (NGSS). Sources are at the end.
  • Ages: UK Year 3 and Cambridge Stage 3 are about age 7 to 8. US grade 3 is about age 8 to 9.

Ask Buddy (122 spoken answers, plus 11 on Ghana and Africa)

Buddy's short answers back up the units above. A child can ask, for example, "How do magnets work?" (Year 3), "Why is the sea salty?" (Cambridge 5ESp.02), "How does electricity work?" (Year 4) or "What are the phases of the Moon?" (Cambridge 6ESs.02). The Ghana and Africa answers (items 547–557) support geography and world-cultures work, such as the Nile, the Sahara, Kilimanjaro, Mansa Musa and Great Zimbabwe. They match no single science objective.
