Curious Buddy: earning plan

How to earn from the app without ads: selling to schools first, then an optional free-to-try version for families. A plan to decide from. Nothing here changes the app yet.

The prices below are starting points, not facts. I don't know what schools in your area pay for learning materials today. Use them to start conversations, then adjust after talking to 3 to 5 schools. Check the tax and legal side with an accountant before charging.

Part 1: Selling to schools

Why schools first

  • One sale covers many children.
  • Schools value what Curious Buddy already has: it works offline, it's safe, it matches the curriculum, and teachers can check every fact.
  • No app changes are needed to start. You can take payment by mobile money or bank transfer, so no app store takes a cut.

A simple way to anchor the price in conversation: "less per pupil each year than one exercise book a term".

How to sell, step by step

  1. Finish testing on the phone (the phone checklist), and test the final release version.
  2. Fill in the school pack: your name and contact in the pitch and safety statement, and have a teacher check the curriculum map.
  3. Free pilots: 2 or 3 schools, one term. Before and after, ask pupils 10 short quiz questions on paper, to show what they learned.
  4. Collect evidence: teacher quotes, pupil reactions (no names or photos without the school's written permission) and the quiz results.
  5. Offer paid packages to the pilot schools first, then to nearby schools, using the evidence.
  6. Look for sponsors for schools that can't pay, using the same evidence.

Part 2: Free to try, for families (later)

  • 3 Mystery Missions
  • Quiz Time: starter level of every topic
  • 5 games, including Dino Discovery
  • Creative Island
  • All Living Stories and Mystery Missions
  • All quiz levels
  • All games
  • Everything added in future, at no extra cost

Safety and kindness come first. Buddy's answers, the safety replies and the feelings replies are never locked.

The rules I'd follow

  • One fair price, paid once. No subscriptions, coins, gems, timers or "pay to continue".
  • Only grown-ups can buy. The purchase sits behind the Grown-ups check, and Google Play also asks for the account password.
  • Children are never pressured. Locked items show a calm "Ask a grown-up" note, never a countdown or a pop-up sale.
  • Restore purchases on a new phone.
  • The internet is only used at the moment of buying. After that, the app works offline as now.

What it would take

  • A Google Play developer account (a one-time fee) and a payments profile. Check that Google Play supports paid apps and payouts in your country.
  • Google Play Billing in the app. That is a new library, a new permission and an internet connection at purchase time, so it changes the privacy statement.
  • A privacy policy that covers purchases.
  • Google's rules for children's apps (the Families policy).
  • Google keeps a share of each sale, usually 15% for small developers.

Part 3: Other income

  • Sponsors and grants: education charities, NGOs, government programmes, telecoms and banks. Thank them with "Supported by…" in the Grown-ups area only.
  • Printed books: Buddy activity books and storybooks, using the app's own stories and facts.
  • Teacher packs: worksheets and lesson guides, sold to schools.
  • Language versions: Twi, Ga, Ewe or French, paid for by schools or organisations.

Never

  • Ads of any kind.
  • Selling or collecting children's data.
  • Subscriptions children can trigger, loot boxes, timers or pressure.
